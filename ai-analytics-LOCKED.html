04Why the prompt is the whole architecture

Everything that made this work was telling the model true things about your business up front. Nothing that checked its work afterwards helped.

A worked example of why this matters

Before: "doctor wise how many cases last month" joined the wrong table — ClinicDoctor (the consultant who sees the patient) instead of ReferralDoctor (who sends you business). It returned 2, 3, 12 where the truth is 59, 83, 106, 145, 245.

Small, plausible numbers about entirely the wrong people. No amount of checking the SQL finds that — the query was valid. One line in the vocabulary block fixed it permanently.

05Why it can't just check its own work

The single most counter-intuitive result, and the reason there is no self-correcting loop.

It once answered "last month's profit was ₹11,96,252.28."

Real columns. Correct arithmetic. Clean joins. That figure is revenue minus referral commission minus clinic payouts — a perfectly good number that simply is not profit, because your system records no salaries, rent, consumables or lab costs.

There is no bug in that query for a checker to find. The SQL is valid, the maths is right, the result is plausible. It's wrong because of something only you know about your business. That is why the weight of this design sits in the prompt, not in a verifier.

Three ways of checking were tried. All measured.

ApproachResult
Ask the model to inspect its own answerno gain and the measurement was itself flawed
Flag a suspicious answer and rewrite itfixed nothing across three runs — a second attempt repeats the same misunderstanding
Write the query 3 times and take the majorityidentical score at 3× the cost
Write it twice and flag a disagreementkept — it can't fix the answer, but it can tell you which ones to check

Why rewriting fails: the model isn't randomly wrong, it's consistently wrong. Ask ten times and it misunderstands the same thing ten times. Like asking someone to re-total a column when they keep misreading the same digit — you need to look at the column, not ask again.

So it flags instead of fixing. On money and ranking questions only, it writes the query a second time and compares the answers. If they disagree you still get the number, with a line under it.

It fires on about 1 answer in 8. When it fires, the answer really is wrong half the time — against a background rate of 22%. It bothers you on only 7% of correct answers.

Why not check everything? A warning you see constantly is a warning you stop reading. The fixed-rule checks were dropped for exactly this — they fired on correct answers four times out of five, which is worse than saying nothing.

The hard set is the honest one. It was written after the system was built, deliberately targeting things it had never been shown — retention, medians, "who stopped referring", days with no activity. It gets about 25 of 32 right and 7 wrong. Roughly 2 of those 7 are flaws in my own test questions rather than the system.

Run-to-run variation is ±2 questions, so treat any difference smaller than that as noise. Several conclusions during this project had to be withdrawn for ignoring exactly this.

07Where it stops

  • An empty field reads as zero. Ask "how many bills used a coupon" and it says 0 — but that field is never filled in, so 0 means not recorded, not none. Profit is handled specially; the general case is not solved.
  • It cannot tell you what it got wrong. When two attempts disagree it flags the answer; it cannot say which one is right.
  • Anything outside your own records — competitors, market share, where a patient went instead — is refused rather than guessed.
  • Costs aren't in the system, so real profit is unanswerable until they are.
