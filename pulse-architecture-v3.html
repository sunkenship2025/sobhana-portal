The one rule

Nothing may match words against the question to decide how to answer.

Two keyword ladders were built here and both failed within an hour of being written. The first chose the chart; the second chose the prose contract. Both are gone.

"break down discounts in the last 30 days by reason"
Matched the trend pattern before breakdown was tested — because of the words "last 30 days" — and a split question rendered as a time series.
What replaced it
The analyst declares the job. The system computes the structure. A deterministic matcher scores the truthful options. No extra model call, and a phrase nobody anticipated cannot break it.

The worst case is the most open-ended "why" question, and it is seven sequential model calls at roughly thirteen seconds each — model latency, not loop overhead. A per-step timeout was tried and removed: it did not move the worst case at all, and it turned a slow-but-correct dues count into a refusal. A guard that cannot help but can refuse a good answer is worse than no guard.

The pattern underneath

Nearly every defect was an assumption that became a fact because nobody checked it.

A required artifact that rendered nothing. A validator enforcing a constraint it never verified was real. A "complete list" missing a debtor. A feature reported as non-existent while 44,743 rows of it sat readable. A benchmark declared lost that was sitting in the repo root.

The fixes that held share one shape: read the values, not the names. Numeric detection that stripped non-digits turned every text column into a measure. Dimensions detected from a name whitelist scored zero on a column literally called reason. Money identified by column name missed netBilledAfterDiscount. The same mistake wearing different clothes.

Verify absence. Never infer it.
