Background removal: what ships vs the middle ground

Left column is the simulated upload. Middle is what ships today (flat-field: blur the page to estimate paper, divide, threshold). Right is Sauvola local thresholding with hysteresis — the standard document-binarisation method, same idea but the threshold also adapts to local contrast, and faint strokes survive if they touch a confident one. Both run here, in your browser, on the same pixels.

CaseUploadedShips today — flat-fieldMiddle ground — Sauvola + hysteresis
What Sauvola adds. Threshold per pixel is T = m(1 + k(s/R - 1)) where m and s are the local mean and standard deviation. On blank paper s is near zero, so T drops well below the paper and noise cannot become ink — that is the flat-field method's weak spot. Both means come from blurred copies (E[x] and E[x²]), so it is still two canvas passes and no dependency.
What hysteresis adds. Two thresholds instead of one: pixels well under T are seeds, pixels just under it survive only if 8-connected to a seed. Faint stroke tails stay attached; isolated paper grain and dust die. Costs one flood fill.
What neither fixes. Ruled lines and printed form text are ink to any thresholder. That needs morphological line removal (detect long thin runs and subtract), which is another ~30 lines — worth adding only if signatures actually arrive on ruled paper.
